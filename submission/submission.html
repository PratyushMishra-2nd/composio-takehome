100 apps, one agent: what it takes to turn an app into an agent toolkit

An agent pipeline researched auth, credential gates, API surface and MCP coverage for all 100 apps, citing a verbatim quote for every answer, then verified itself with non-LLM checks, a browser-use loop and a blind sample check, and opened the follow-up queue through Composio.

The patterns

1
Half are easy wins: 50 of 100 can ship today

41 more with a caveat; only 9 need outreach or have no usable API.

2
Most common blocker: money, then vendor review

17 need a trial or paid plan, 10 need vendor app review, 8 are customer-only, 2 need a partnership. Every app but one has a documented API.

3
Friction clusters in sales-led SaaS and ad platforms

Support and finance tools are often customer-only (DealCloud, Gladly, Pylon, Brex, PitchBook); ads sit behind app review (Google, Meta, LinkedIn). Dev-infra and productivity are nearly all build-now.

4
Toolkits need both auth modes: 56 offer OAuth2 and a key

OAuth2 in 67, OAuth-only in 10; key-only apps (25) cluster in data/SEO and AI-native tools.

5
37 build-now apps publish a spec: generation candidates

64 publish OpenAPI/GraphQL (51 confirmed), 81 have webhooks for triggers, 57 a free sandbox. AI-native tools are least ready (4/10 webhooks).

6
For Composio: 19 gaps, 6 buildable now

81/100 already have a toolkit (via Composio MCP). Quick adds: Vonage, systeme.io, Ecwid, fanbasis, Binance, Reducto; 3 have a spec (Vonage, Binance, Reducto).
